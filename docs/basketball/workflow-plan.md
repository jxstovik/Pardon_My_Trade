## The chosen strategy and available pivots

The forecasting layer estimates the same player/game distributions for every strategy. The strategy layer changes roster construction preferences, risk, horizon, protected assets and tactical targets. It never changes league scoring or inflates projections to fit a story. Every profile is saved with a version and effective period. **Balanced is the initial persistent profile**; no additional strategy selection is needed to start implementation.

| Persistent profile | Approach in this eight-category league | Main tradeoff and pivot evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Balanced** — selected | Seek category gains across all eight, preserve a strong core and slot flexibility, and compare the next effective week, 14 days and season value. No intentional punt. | Stay when alternatives depend on fragile minutes/availability assumptions or sacrifice recurring category wins for a small local edge. |
| **Bigs** | Prefer a roster core with strong REB, BLK and attempt-weighted FG%, while evaluating PTS, ST and available complementary guards. | Could weaken AST, 3PTM or FT%. Switch only when the actual roster/pool and opponent comparisons show a durable net category/season gain after those losses and transaction costs. A persistent FT% or other punt requires a separate user choice. |
| **Guards** | Prefer AST, 3PTM, FT% and scoring opportunities; retain enough frontcourt value and legal F/C depth. Turnovers receive no invented penalty. | Could weaken REB, BLK or FG%. Switch only after the same full eight-category comparison and roster-transition costs. No automatic big-man fire sale. |

All profiles retain the common reporting metric `E[category wins] = Σ P(win category c)`, alongside expected ties/losses. Preferred categories guide construction/tiebreaks and scenario exploration; a favored-category subtotal never masquerades as improved league value. Compare each candidate policy against keeping the current policy on the **same immutable snapshot, legal starts and model**.

Use temporary overlays rather than repeatedly rebuilding the base strategy:

| Overlay | Trigger and behavior | Expiry / constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Schedule pressure** | Extra *usable* games improve expected category outcomes after FG%/FT% exposure. Compare full effective weeks and replacement fringe players. | Usually one effective period. Weekly execution and waiver activation can make extra scheduled games unusable; do not stream into locked lineups. |
| **Quality / development** | Either prefer reliable minutes/availability and percentage contribution when starts are scarce, or use replaceable reserves for evidence-backed emerging roles. Compare each alternative separately. | Recheck weekly. Lower variance is not automatically better; a speculative reserve has holding cost and no IR slot is available. |
| **Must-win** | Qualification/elimination scenarios or an explicit user choice justify concentrating resources on reachable current categories or playoff advancement. | Ends with that period. Protect the core and show future category/seed cost; spending more does not guarantee a better result. |
| **Playoff runway** | Qualification is sufficiently secure to favor the actual P18/P19 school schedules and bracket/seed value. | Recheck every period and return to Balanced if qualification cost dominates. Conditional tournament games stay conditional; NCAA tournament games are not assumed to count. |

Every strategy preview reports current/candidate profile and overlay; eight category distributions and W/L/T probabilities **when validated**; change in expected category wins; expected ties/losses; makes/attempts and percentage exposure; legal counted starts; qualification/seeding/playoff sensitivity when supported; waiver-priority and dropped-player opportunity cost; source age, uncertainty and next deadline. Before calibration or complete opponents, show transparent totals/scenario ranges and mark probability fields unavailable.

Propose a persistent pivot only if its advantage survives plausible injury/minutes cases and is material under a versioned threshold selected before evaluation. Do not hard-code a spurious percentage threshold before measuring model noise. The user chooses persistent pivots; tactical overlays expire by default. A profile change produces a new analysis and invalidates incompatible proposals. It never submits a move.

## Data and model roadmap

1. **League contract and truthful capture.** Keep basketball rules, source states, periods, rosters and strategy schemas separate from football. Unknown, zero, empty, unlimited and unsupported are different values. Import official raw JSON/capture bundles with hashes, observation times, source URLs, parser versions and completeness. Record a transcription as a transcription: its hash authenticates only those bytes; the original-payload hash remains unavailable unless captured. Current browser reads work; shell HTTP returned 403. Do not advertise an unattended live reader until a permitted transport succeeds repeatedly. Do not circumvent access controls or store session secrets.
2. **Identity, schedules and outcomes.** Maintain canonical players and school/team-season stints with Fantrax/provider crosswalks; quarantine ambiguous matches. Use stable game IDs, tip instants, postponement/correction lineage and fantasy period assignments. Missing box scores are missing, not phantom zero games. Retain source-as-of times and immutable raw observations.
3. **A transparent baseline.** Forecast availability, minutes conditional on playing and box-stat rates with partial pooling/recency calibrated on earlier data. Explicit cold-start priors cover freshmen/transfers/uncovered players. Use permitted timestamped platform projections as a benchmark if available. Prioritize correct schedules, minutes, eligibility and legal weekly starts before complex ML.
4. **Exact categories and legal optimization.** Sum PTS/REB/AST/ST/BLK/3PTM over counted games; compute FG% as total FGM/FGA and FT% as total FTM/FTA. Honor verified minimums, zero-attempt, rounding/ties, locks and caps. Test total-game/attempt accounting. Simulate coherent makes/attempts, correlated pace/minutes and both teams before producing category probabilities. More games can damage percentages; a player on a crowded day may add no counted start.
5. **Research and validation.** Rolling-origin time splits plus a held-out recent season; no random player-game split. Freeze input availability before each decision. Fit priors, transforms, calibration and ensemble weights within training folds. Publish minutes/stat error, probabilistic scoring, coverage, category calibration and decision costs by cold-start/transfer/status cohort. Historical archives lacking as-of evidence support retrospective studies, not a claim of executable historical profit.
6. **Personal model with promotion gates.** Add a hierarchical minutes/rates distribution first; compare nonlinear challengers only if they improve preregistered metrics. Keep candidate artifacts and active-model pointer separate. Require scoring/data correctness, replayability/no leakage, meaningful held-out improvement without unacceptable calibration/cohort regression, real shadow cycles, failure recovery and rollback. Archive every forecast before outcomes. Keep the healthy baseline available.

Each run pins rules, roster, identity map, schedule/status observations, model/training cutoff, strategy, optimizer and source ages in an immutable manifest. Corrections create new observations; refreshing projections never changes the meaning of an old recommendation.

Freshness includes source observation age and ingestion age. Reimporting the October 25 evidence tomorrow does not refresh the roster or draft state. The existing football updater can save models after a held/failed promotion decision, and its repository can attach latest projections to old snapshots; neither behavior is reused for basketball. Game-level candidates and pinned projection manifests remain isolated.

## Boundaries and evidence of success

Automatically acquire permitted reads, validate, analyze, compare strategies, archive forecasts/results and prepare reports as capabilities become available. The existing football database, queue, calendar and `--auto` behavior must not be inherited. Basketball has an independent league-scoped store and no successful-looking execution stub.

Claims, drops and trades require exact human approval, separate from submission; protected-player checks persist. Future low-risk owned-player slot automation requires explicit enabled policy plus a supported verified Fantrax writer, known locks and fresh-state checks. Changing strategy, training a model or scheduling a task provides no transaction authority. Any uncertain submission result requires reconciliation before retry.

Measure actual illegal/missed-start recommendations, stale required inputs, failed/missed jobs, duplicate notifications, controllable bench opportunity cost, source/player coverage, forecast calibration and decision cost; do not present zero-error targets as achieved results. Keep user attention to one routine digest plus material exceptions and exact high-impact decisions. Report implementation, real-data readiness, scheduled execution and model quality separately so a passing synthetic example cannot be mistaken for a working live manager.
